# Proposed Next Phase: Eastern Nile Delta Paleohydrology

## Status

**Proposed methodological extension. Not yet validated.**

This note documents a proposed next phase of the Hydrological Thresholds research program. The completed controlled validation currently applies to the Yabboq / Wadi Zarqa basin in the Southern Levant.

The eastern Nile Delta work described here has not yet been run as a validated paleohydrological reconstruction.

## Research Question

To what extent can terrain-derived hydrological modeling recover relict channel structure in an avulsing deltaic landscape before geophysical or sedimentary evidence is introduced?

The eastern Nile Delta provides a substantially different geomorphic environment from the bedrock-constrained Yabboq basin. Channel migration, avulsion, aggradation, infilling, subsidence, and later agricultural modification require a separate calibration framework.

## Why Calibration Is Necessary

A terrain-first workflow that performs well in a confined drainage basin cannot simply be transferred to a low-gradient delta plain without testing how much ancient channel structure remains detectable in modern terrain.

The proposed workflow therefore begins with documented analog controls rather than the target landscape itself.

## Proposed Control Framework

The calibration suite will use known abandoned or relict distributary systems whose former courses are independently documented.

Candidate controls include:

- Pelusiac and Tanitic channel belts near Bubastis and the Tineh Plain, Nile Delta
- Ba Lai distributary, Mekong Delta
- Bayou Lafourche / Bayou Teche relict channels, Mississippi Delta
- documented Yellow River Delta avulsions, including Diaokou and Qingshuigou

For each control:

1. Withhold the known paleochannel geometry from the terrain model.
2. Derive candidate drainage structure from modern elevation data.
3. Compare model output against independently documented paleochannel evidence.
4. Measure recovery rate, spatial correspondence, and false-positive behavior.
5. Evaluate how performance changes across geomorphic settings and spatial tolerances.

## Model-First, Core-Verified

The proposed workflow does not treat DEM-derived drainage as a replacement for geoarchaeological field evidence.

Its purpose is to evaluate whether terrain analysis can serve as a regional predictive tool for identifying high-probability paleochannel corridors that can later be tested using:

- sediment cores
- geophysics
- published paleochannel reconstructions
- historical Survey of Egypt mapping
- archaeological excavation data
- geomorphological and sedimentological evidence

## Proposed Eastern Nile Delta Application

The initial target area is the broader Tell el-Dabʿa / Avaris landscape in the eastern Nile Delta.

Avaris is treated here as a methodological test area, not as a completed reconstruction.

No claim is currently made that:

- a specific Bronze Age channel has been recovered
- a biblical migration route has been identified
- a Reed Sea crossing location has been established
- DEM-derived flow alone can reconstruct ancient Delta hydrology

The first task is calibration.

Only if the workflow demonstrates recoverable signal in documented control deltas should the calibrated method be transferred to the Avaris study area.

## Relationship to the Yabboq Validation

The Yabboq / Wadi Zarqa validation established that terrain-derived drainage corresponded with independently mapped hydrography more strongly than random and terrain-matched control expectations across multiple spatial tolerances.

That result applies to the tested Yabboq landscape.

The eastern Nile Delta extension asks a different question:

**How much hydrological structure remains recoverable when the landscape itself is avulsing, depositional, and heavily modified?**

This proposed phase is designed to make that question falsifiable before historical interpretation enters the model.

## Open Questions

Key methodological questions include:

- Which delta analogs provide the most appropriate geomorphic controls?
- What DEM resolution is sufficient for detecting relict distributary structure?
- How should modern agricultural leveling and canal construction be masked or modeled?
- Which recovery metrics best distinguish paleochannel signal from generic low-relief terrain?
- Can topology-preserving controls improve on cell-based null models?
- How should uncertainty propagate from control calibration into the target landscape?

## Reproducibility

Code, documentation, figures, and future control outputs will remain version-controlled within this repository.

Validated results and proposed extensions will continue to be explicitly separated.

The goal is not to make GIS prove a historical narrative.

The goal is to make geographic claims vulnerable to geographic evidence.
